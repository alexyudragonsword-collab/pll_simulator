pllsim v0.8.0 — TDC nonlinearity, BBPD metastability, pulling, corners

Four impairments that were absent rather than wrong.  No preset number
changes: everything here defaults off, and the presets declare none of
it.  What changes is what you can ask.

## TDC INL now predicts its own spurs

The TDC-based ADPLL used to hand back an empty spur table on a
fractional channel and a note pointing at simulate().  That was a real
gap, not a modelling limit: with a fractional FCW the TDC input sweeps
its range at the beat rate, so the normalized code is x[n] = (n*frac)
mod 1 and a declared INL is a fixed function sampled along that ramp.
Expanding it in a Fourier series on [0,1) maps harmonic k to a tone at
fold(k*frac)*fref.

New `core.tdcspurs`, mirroring what `core.dtcspurs` does for the DTC
path.  A whole number of INL cycles gives exactly one tone, which is the
easiest case to check by hand and is pinned as such.  With no INL
declared the note now says so explicitly — an ideal TDC's beats come
from quantization alone, which remains a simulate() measurement.

## BBPD metastability, in closed form

`ADPLLConfig.bb_meta_window_s` is the sampling flop's resolution window.
Inside it the flop cannot resolve in the time available and the decision
is a coin flip — but it still emits +/-1.  That distinction is the whole
result: output power is unchanged, so the entire effect lands on the
gain.

With input jitter sigma the averaged characteristic is
E[out|dt] = Q((W-dt)/sigma) - Q((W+dt)/sigma), whose slope at the origin
divided by the W=0 value sqrt(2/pi)/sigma leaves

    Kbb(W)/Kbb(0) = exp(-W^2 / (2 sigma^2)) .

A window equal to sigma_t costs 4.34 dB of detector gain and widens the
input-referred noise by exactly the same factor.  The self-consistent
linearization applies it inside its own iteration, so the loop bandwidth
moves too.  Checked against 200k Monte Carlo samples of the real
comparator to 5%.

## Injection pulling, on all six architectures

`OscConfig.pull_lock_range_hz` / `pull_offset_hz`.  In the weak-pulling
limit of Adler's equation the oscillator carries a residual FM of peak
deviation f_L at the beat rate, so the sideband is f_L/(2*df) before the
loop suppresses whatever falls in band.

This is coupling into the tank, not into the loop, which is why it
applies to every architecture including the injection-locked ones — and
why chasing it in the loop filter does not work.  Both are now tested:
in band the loop rejects it; past the bandwidth the reported sideband is
the bare f_L/(2*df), untouched.

`OscConfig.lock_range_from_tank(f0, i_ratio, q)` computes f_L the usual
way.  An aggressor *inside* the lock range reports no sideband at all
and says why: the oscillator is captured, not pulled, and the
weak-pulling expression describes nothing there.

Cross-checked analytic against time domain on all seven presets, within
1 dB.  For the ILCM and MDLL the comparison is made against the record
the expression describes — the phase error at the injection instants.
An oversampled record additionally carries the intra-period ramp, worth
+4.4 dB on the ILCM at 0.2*fref, which is the same ref-rate-versus-
oversampled distinction their jitter numbers already carry.

## Named PVT corners

New `pllsim.corners`.  Monte Carlo answers "what is the yield"; it does
not answer the question a design review asks first, "does it still work
at SS/125C/0.9*VDD", because that is a small set of named reproducible
points rather than a sample.  A loop can pass one and fail the other.

`Corner` carries multiplicative deviations on the quantities a PDK
corner actually moves — Kvco, f0, Icp, resistors, capacitors, gm,
digital delay — plus temperature, supply, and an oscillator phase-noise
offset in dB.  `apply_corner` rebuilds the config and deliberately does
NOT retune the loop, because the question is what the nominal design
does there.  Fields absent from an architecture are skipped, so one
corner applies to all six with no per-architecture table.

`STANDARD_CORNERS` is TT/SS-hot/FF-cold/SF-hot/FS-cold.  On
cppll_19p2m_4p8g the unity-gain bandwidth spans 622 kHz to 1345 kHz
across them — a 2.2x spread the nominal number does not show.

The phase-noise axis is there because without it the injection-locked
multipliers do not move at all: their bandwidth is fref/pi by
construction, so scaling passives leaves them byte-identical at every
corner, which reads as immunity rather than as an unmodelled axis.
Every architecture is now tested to respond to a corner.

A corner that will not build is reported as a row with its exception,
not allowed to abort the sweep — a control voltage that cannot reach the
target at SS is a finding, and losing the other four corners to it would
be the wrong trade.
