pllsim v0.7.0 — the reference spur, made observable and made right

v0.6.0 fixed a 6 dB error in the reference-spur expression and noted, in
the test that pinned it, that "nothing in the time domain can check
this".  That was true of the engines as they stood and it was the wrong
thing to accept: a convention no test can reach is a convention that
drifts.  This release makes the reference spur observable in the time
domain for all three analog architectures, and the cross-check
immediately found the expression still wrong — this time by 38 dB, in
the opposite direction, and for a reason worth understanding.

### Why the charge-pump spur dropped 36 dB

The model treated the per-period impairment charge as a single impulse
of area dq, giving a fundamental of peak amplitude 2*dq*fref.  That is
right for leakage and wrong for mismatch, and the difference is the
loop itself.

A type-II loop has infinite DC gain, so in lock it has *already* driven
the net per-period charge to zero — it sits at whatever static timing
offset makes that true.  What remains is not one impulse but two of
opposite sign: one source conducts alone for |dt|, then both conduct
for t_reset.  They cancel in area.  Their fref component survives only
through their separation in time, a factor 2*sin(pi*fref*dt_sep) below
the single-impulse answer.  For a 200 ps reset pulse at 19.2 MHz that
is 38 dB.

Leakage escapes this because a constant current has no fref component
at all: the entire fundamental comes from the narrow correction pulse,
one impulse, and the old expression was exactly right.  Both cases now
fall out of one segment-level Fourier sum with no special-casing —
`ChargePump.ripple_fundamental_a`.

Cross-checked against the time domain at three mismatch/leakage
combinations, agreeing to within 1 dB.

### Why the sub-sampling architectures no longer report one

The SSPLL/SPLL spur was computed from the sampling pedestal.  The
pedestal adds to the *held* voltage, and the gm converts that same held
voltage to charge over that same window — so a type-II loop parks where
`A*sin(perr) + pedestal = 0` and the gm then delivers no charge at all.
No charge is no ripple.  A static pedestal produces a static phase
offset and nothing else.

That is not a modelling shortcut; it is a real property of the
architecture, and a large part of why sub-sampling loops are known for
clean reference spurs.  Confirmed directly: with an oversampled record
these presets show -276 dBc at fref, i.e. nothing.

The mechanism that does survive is the sampling clock's kickback, which
lands at a *different* instant from the gm pulse that cancels it.  New
`SamplerConfig.kick_q_c` / `kick_delay_s` model it, in both domains
(analytic -91.34 dBc vs time-domain -91.34 dBc on spll_100m_8g).  The
presets ship with no kickback, so they report no reference spur and say
why in `analyze().notes` — inventing a kickback charge to keep a number
on the page would be fabricating the measurement.

`analyze()` no longer reports -600 dBc for an absent spur anywhere; an
absence is an omitted key, not a number that looks like a measurement.

## New: the reference spur in the time domain

`CPPLL/SSPLL/SPLL.simulate(fine_oversample=M)` records M control-voltage
samples per reference period instead of one.  At M=1 — still the default,
so nothing changes unasked — the control ripple sits exactly at the
record's own sampling rate and aliases to DC, which is why analyze() had
been predicting a spur the time domain could not show.

`LoopFilter.drive_fine` applies a piecewise-constant current waveform
within one step, so the up/down segments are resolved rather than lumped
into one net pulse.  `ChargePump.segments` and `SamplingPD.segments`
supply the waveforms.  ILCM and MDLL, which already had oversampled
records, now share the same post-processing (`arch.base.attach_fine`).

M has to resolve the reset pulse or the spur is under-read; the engine
says so in `notes` when it does not.

## New: impairments that were missing

- **CP mismatch and leakage vs control voltage** (`mismatch_slope_pct_v`,
  `leakage_slope_a_v`, `v_ref`).  A constant mismatch predicts one spur
  for the whole tuning range; silicon shows a V-shape with a minimum at
  the crossing voltage.  `CPPLL.ref_spur_vs_channel()` traces it.
- **PFD dead zone** (`dead_zone_s`).  Suppresses the error-dependent
  charge inside the window, leaving the mismatch charge flowing.  The
  loop is gainless there and wanders; analyze() says plainly that its
  linear model overstates in-band rejection when one is configured.
- **Tri-state PFD wrapping** (`pfd_mode="wrap"`).  The existing model
  clamps at +/-0.45*Tref, which pulls in monotonically and never slips.
  A real tri-state PFD is linear over +/-2pi and periodic in 4pi, so a
  large frequency error drives it through its own reversal.  Runs report
  `cycle_slips` and `pfd_out_of_range_cycles` separately — a saturating
  detector cannot slip, and calling both the same thing hid that.
  Default stays "clamp".
- **Divider retiming** (`divider_retimed`, `retime_jitter_rms_s`).  A
  VCO-clocked flop discards the divider chain's accumulated jitter and
  substitutes its own aperture jitter, in both domains.
- **Lock detector** (`blocks.lockdetect`).  The window comparator and
  asymmetric up/down counter a chip actually reports LOCK from, as
  opposed to a frequency-error threshold applied afterwards with
  hindsight.  Reports first-assert time, locked fraction and unlock
  events.
